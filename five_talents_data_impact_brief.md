# Five Talents: Using Data for Impact in Burundi

**Focus:** Women’s literacy, financial inclusion and community-owned savings groups  
**Research source:** CommCare (Dimagi), *Five Talents: Savings groups, owned by their communities* / July 2025 case study.

## 1. Who are they?
Five Talents is a nonprofit organisation that supports women-led savings groups across East Africa. Its programmes combine community savings, literacy and training to help participants strengthen financial independence and develop small businesses. In Burundi, its work has connected literacy learning with community-owned savings groups and long-term economic and social empowerment.

## 2. What data do they use?
Five Talents’ field teams use CommCare, a digital data collection platform, to record information about savings-group members, meetings, training and engagement. They also capture qualitative feedback and participants’ stories. Field staff can collect information offline in remote areas and sync it when they regain connectivity. Programme managers use dashboards to monitor participation and engagement, identify gaps or problems early, and verify records. This combines quantitative monitoring data with qualitative feedback.

## 3. What changed because of it?
The published Burundi case study reports that more than **144,000 people** have learned to read and write since the programme began, **78% of them women**. Participants formed more than **6,200 community-owned savings groups**, and **over 84%** reportedly continued operating independently more than ten years after funding ended. The monitoring system also helps programme teams identify participation challenges and adjust support, while the evaluation evidence illustrates the programme’s longer-term sustainability. These impact figures are reported by the case study; they should not be interpreted as proof that data collection alone caused the outcomes.
